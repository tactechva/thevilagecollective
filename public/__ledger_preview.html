The Village Collective

Motto · struck on every leaf Every season of life deserves a village.

How the volume came to be kept

Her cleaning clients kept asking who do you trust. She had answers, because she was already inside their homes. Those referrals became a village.

What was once a name scribbled on the back of an invoice is now ruled, numbered and bound. Nothing in these pages was bought. Nothing in these pages is ranked. An entry is made when a woman with a mop and a good memory can say, out loud, that she would send her own mother.

the wreath is open on purpose — there is still room in it

How to read a ledger page

Every entry carries the same brass key, struck at the same size, in the same ink. There is no star, no tier, no favourite. The only column that separates one trade from another is HOW SERVED — one comes to your house, one keeps a storefront, one keeps a studio, one meets you in an office. That is a difference of shape, never of worth.

Read across the rule, not down it. The account of the business is set in her own words, spelling and all, because a register that tidies people up is no longer a record of them.

      COLOPHON — Vol. I of The Village Collective, a register of trades kept by hand in Hampton Roads, Virginia. Ruled in oxide red and carbon blue on warm paper. Display struck in Ultra; the accounts set in Petrona; the columns lettered in Archivo Narrow; slips typed on a machine that needed a new ribbon.
